01 / THE CHALLENGE

A boundary on a map.
A community behind it.

Flood planning needs more than a location. It needs an understanding of the people within it. Floodsafe explores how GIS and population data can make that first step easier.

The current prototype lets a user select an area, divide it into H3 hexagons, estimate its population, and export the results. It establishes a foundation for future flood-exposure analysis and evacuation planning.

↗

Our aim
Help planners understand potential evacuation demand before connecting that demand to routes, shelters, and resources.

02 / THE CURRENT WORKFLOW

From a place to a
population estimate.

Six steps turn a selected geographic area into structured, exportable spatial data.

  1. 01

    Find a place

    Search for a location and navigate to the relevant area on an interactive map.

  2. 02

    Draw a boundary

    Select a polygon or rectangle around the campus, neighborhood, or area of interest.

  3. 03

    Divide into hexagons

    Use H3 spatial indexing to organize the selected area into smaller analysis cells.

  4. 04

    Estimate population

    Use population-density data. WorldPop retrieval is intended; an assumed demo density provides an offline fallback.

  5. 05

    Explore the map

    Visualize estimated distribution. Population markers are illustrative, not individual people’s locations.

  6. 06

    Export the results

    Review the results table and download a CSV for further analysis and comparison.

⬡

Why hexagons?

A single total hides local differences. H3 cells give each small area a spatial identity, helping organize estimates and creating a common grid for future flood-exposure comparisons.

05 / THE SYSTEM VISION

Better context.
More informed decisions.

The architecture extends beyond today’s population prototype. These capabilities are proposed and require implementation and validation.

INPUTS

Understand the terrain

Population grids, roads, shelters, elevation, historical inundation, rainfall, and water-level observations.

  • Geographic and population data
  • Weather and terrain context
ANALYSIS

Evaluate the options

Combine flood-exposure modeling with route accessibility and shelter-capacity allocation.

  • Risk-aware route evaluation
  • Capacity-aware allocation
COORDINATION

Support human action

Present suggested routes and rescue priorities to decision-makers, with human authorization for public alerts.

  • Command-center decision support
  • Human-approved communication
Explore the proposed architecture +

Forecast and spatial context

The design proposes weather ingestion, elevation and historical inundation data, and an ML model such as XGBoost. PostgreSQL with PostGIS would hold roads, shelter locations, and spatial data.

Routing and allocation

The proposed engine would penalize risky routes, exclude inaccessible roads, allocate shelter capacity, and place stranded locations into a rescue queue. Routing rules and thresholds would need local validation.

Communication and oversight

The written architecture proposes geofenced CAP messages for cell broadcast, explicitly authorized by a human official. Shelter updates and field reports would inform the planning view.

Design note: the supplied diagram shows an earlier SMS delivery concept. This overview follows the written architecture’s later cell-broadcast proposal. Neither communication system is represented as operational.

RESEARCH STATUS

Promising direction. Important limits.

Floodsafe is an early-stage prototype, not a validated operational evacuation system. Estimates depend on data quality, coverage, and resolution. Offline mode uses assumed density. There is no validated flood-depth prediction, real-time forecasting, verified evacuation routing, or live tracking of evacuees. The live WorldPop integration still needs testing.
